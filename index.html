When Should a Moderator Speak?

Benchmarking Policy-Grounded Mediation in Full-Duplex Debates

01The task

A full-duplex speech model takes the moderator's seat in a three-person debate. The two debaters' audio plays from start to finish and is never modified; only the moderator's channel is open. The model decides when to speak and what to say — and most of the time the right answer is to stay quiet.

02Ten moderator actions

A covers time and the floor, B covers content and can apply throughout the debate. Each action has a condition that must hold; speaking without it costs points, and so does missing it.

A4Ten-second cue

whenA thirty-second opening or closing statement passes 20 s.

saySay ten seconds are left, over the speaker, without stopping them.

A4xfCrossfire ten-second cue

whenThe two-and-a-half-minute crossfire reaches 2:20.

saySay ten seconds remain in crossfire, without ending the round.

A2-2Hand over after a clean finish

whenThe speaker finished inside thirty seconds on their own.

sayGive the floor to the other side by name, side, or “next”.

A3-1Open the crossfire

whenBoth opening statements are done.

sayAnnounce the move to the next round and state its length.

A3-2Open the closing round

whenThe crossfire has run its two and a half minutes.

sayCut in and move the debate to the closing statements.

A1Cut an overrun

whenThe last speaker of a round passes thirty seconds.

sayStop them for time. Nobody is next, so there is no hand-off.

A2-1Cut, then hand over

whenThe first speaker of a round passes thirty seconds.

sayStop them for time and give the floor to the other side.

A5Block an interruption

whenSomeone cuts into an opening or closing that is not theirs.

sayRestrain the interrupter and return the floor.

B1Back to the motion

whenA speaker leaves the motion and does not come back alone.

sayRedirect them to the motion.

B2Point out a contradiction

whenOne speaker makes two claims that cannot both be true.

sayName the contradiction and ask them to reconcile it. Do not rule.
